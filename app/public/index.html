The 12 indicators
Statistics glossary
Trend
A steady rise or fall over many years, beyond the normal ups and downs from one year to the next. We test straight-line (linear) trends.
Rate of change (Sen's slope)
How much the indicator changes per year. Sen's slope is the middle value of the slopes between every pair of years, so one extreme year (like 2022) cannot dominate it.
Mann-Kendall test
Counts how often later years are higher than earlier years. It does not assume the data follow a bell curve, which suits rainfall and runoff.
t-test
Tests whether the ordinary least-squares trend line is steeper than chance would produce. We show it next to Mann-Kendall as a cross-check.
p-value
The chance of seeing a trend this strong if nothing were really changing. Below 0.05 is the usual bar for "statistically significant".
95% confidence interval
The range that plausibly contains the true rate of change. If it includes zero, the trend is not significant.
Autocorrelation
When one year resembles the year before (soil moisture has memory). It makes trends look more certain than they are, so p-values are adjusted for it (Yue-Wang lag-1 correction).
FDR (q-value)
When many tests run at once, some pass by luck. The Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction guards against that for the regional comparisons.
Years to detect
An estimate of how long a record must be before a trend of this size can stand out from natural variability (Weatherhead et al., 1998). Sometimes all a trend needs is more time.
Anomaly
How far one year is from the 2001–2025 average for the same region.
JJAS
June to September: the monsoon and peak-melt season, when Indus floods happen.
Method and limitations

Seasonal values (June–September, or May–September for snow) were computed for each year 2001–2025 for the Indus Basin (HydroBASINS) and for three regional splits. Trends use Sen's slope and the Mann-Kendall test, with a lag-1 autocorrelation adjustment and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control for regional difference tests. The record-length slider recalculates the tests in your browser and is exploratory; headline verdicts always use the science team's validated 2001–2025 results.

Limits. Map colours show the rate of change in each pixel, not significance. FLDAS runoff, soil moisture and snow water equivalent are model estimates, and runoff is not measured river discharge. MODIS land surface temperature is not air temperature. The snow-cover index is not percent snow cover. 25 years is a short climate record. The app explains background trends; it does not predict individual floods.
